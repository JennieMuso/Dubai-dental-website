What is a dental filling?

A dental filling repairs a cavity, small fracture or worn area so the tooth can function comfortably again.

The dentist removes compromised material, prepares the area and places a restoration selected for the tooth and clinical situation. Tooth-coloured composite is commonly used because it can be shaped and shaded to blend with the smile.

When might you need one?

A filling may be recommended when a tooth has decay or a localised defect that can be restored without a crown or more complex treatment.

Only an examination can confirm the cause. Sensitivity and pain can have several explanations, so early assessment is useful.

Possible signs
Sensitivity to sweet, cold or pressure; food trapping; a rough edge; or a visible dark area may justify an examination.
Small fracture
A chipped or cracked area may be suitable for direct restoration when the remaining tooth is stable.
Worn filling
An older restoration may need attention if it has fractured, leaked or no longer supports the tooth effectively.
No symptoms
Early decay can be symptom-free, which is why regular examinations can identify concerns before they become larger.

Your appointment

Most straightforward fillings are completed in one visit, although timing depends on the tooth and the size of the restoration.

Examine the tooth

The dentist checks the tooth and bite and may recommend an X-ray when it is clinically useful.

Keep you comfortable

Local anaesthetic can numb the area when appropriate. Let the team know about sensitivity or previous concerns.

Prepare and restore

The affected area is cleaned and the filling is placed, shaped and polished to support natural function.

Check the bite

Your dentist checks how the teeth meet and explains eating, sensitivity and home-care guidance.

Filling questions

Does getting a filling hurt?

Local anaesthetic can be used to numb the tooth when clinically appropriate. You may notice pressure or vibration; tell your dentist if you feel discomfort.

How long does a filling take?

Many straightforward fillings can be completed in one appointment. Timing depends on the tooth, the size and position of the area and whether more than one tooth is treated.

When can I eat after a filling?

Your dentist will give advice based on the material and anaesthetic used. Avoid biting your lip or cheek while the area remains numb.

How long should a filling last?

Longevity varies with the size and position of the filling, bite forces, diet, oral hygiene and grinding habits. Regular reviews help monitor its condition.
