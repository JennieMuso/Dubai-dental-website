What is a dental implant?

A dental implant is a small fixture placed in the jaw to support a replacement tooth or teeth.

The final result has two connected parts: the implant beneath the gum and a restoration such as a crown, bridge or implant-supported prosthesis above it. Planning considers both from the start.

Are implants suitable for you?

Suitability depends on the whole clinical picture: health, bone, gums, bite, expectations and long-term maintenance.

Oral health
Active decay and gum disease are usually managed before implant placement.
Bone and anatomy
Three-dimensional imaging may be recommended to assess available bone and nearby structures.
General health
Medical conditions, medicines, smoking and healing factors are reviewed as part of informed planning.
Maintenance
Implants require daily cleaning and professional review. Long-term care is part of the treatment discussion.

Typical treatment stages

Implant care usually unfolds over several appointments because planning, healing and the final restoration happen in sequence.

Consultation and records

The dentist examines your mouth, discusses goals and reviews imaging to identify suitable options.

Prepare the site

Any required dental, gum or bone treatment is planned before or alongside implant placement.

Place and heal

The implant is placed according to the agreed surgical plan and allowed to heal for the recommended period.

Restore and maintain

The final tooth or teeth are fitted, the bite is reviewed and you receive cleaning and maintenance guidance.

Implant questions

How long does implant treatment take?

Treatment commonly takes several months because assessment, healing and restoration occur in stages. Timing varies with bone, gum health, the number of implants and additional procedures.

Is implant treatment painful?

Local anaesthetic and appropriate comfort measures are used for placement. Post-procedure discomfort and swelling vary; your clinician explains what to expect and how to manage recovery.

Can an implant replace more than one tooth?

Yes. Depending on the situation, implants can support one crown, a bridge replacing several teeth or a larger prosthesis. The number and position are planned individually.

How long do dental implants last?

Implants can provide long-term service, but no lifespan is guaranteed. Health, smoking, cleaning, bite forces and professional maintenance all influence outcomes.
