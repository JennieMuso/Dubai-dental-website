When is oral surgery considered?

Oral surgery may be recommended when a tooth is severely damaged, infected, impacted or cannot be predictably restored.

The dentist first examines the concern and reviews suitable alternatives. If surgery is appropriate, the plan covers anaesthesia, medicines, preparation, expected recovery and options for replacing a tooth when relevant.

Reasons for referral

The right procedure depends on the diagnosis, surrounding anatomy and your medical history—not just the symptom.

Severely damaged tooth
A tooth may require removal when decay or fracture leaves too little healthy structure for predictable restoration.
Wisdom tooth concern
Pain, repeated infection, decay or an impacted position may justify assessment and imaging.
Persistent infection
When a tooth cannot be predictably managed with restorative or root canal treatment, extraction may be discussed.
Orthodontic planning
Occasionally, removal is considered as part of a coordinated orthodontic plan after careful assessment.

From assessment to recovery

Knowing the sequence makes it easier to prepare for the procedure and the first days of healing.

Examination and imaging

The clinician reviews the tooth, nearby structures and relevant medical information before confirming the plan.

Preparation

You receive instructions about meals, medicines, transport or an escort when any of these are relevant to your care.

Procedure

Local anaesthetic is commonly used, with other comfort options discussed when clinically appropriate.

Aftercare and review

You receive guidance for bleeding, cleaning, eating, activity, medicines and signs that require a call to the clinic.

Oral surgery questions

How long does recovery take?

Early healing often occurs over the first one to two weeks, but recovery varies with the tooth, procedure and individual health. Follow your clinician’s personalised instructions.

What should I eat after an extraction?

Soft, cool foods are commonly advised at first. Avoid disturbing the area and follow the clinic’s guidance on eating, drinking, brushing and medicines.

When should I call after surgery?

Contact the clinic if bleeding does not settle as instructed, swelling or pain worsens unexpectedly, you develop a fever or you are concerned about healing.

Will I need to replace the tooth?

That depends on the tooth and your bite. If replacement is useful, the dentist can discuss implants, bridges or other options and the appropriate timing.
